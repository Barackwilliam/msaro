5 Days
Marangu Route

Known as the "Coca-Cola" route, Marangu is the only Kilimanjaro trail with mountain huts instead of tents, making it the most comfortable option. The gradient is gentle and the path well-trodden, but the fast ascent profile means a lower summit success rate unless extended to 6 days — we always recommend the extra day for proper acclimatization.

Full Day-by-Day Itinerary
1
Marangu Gate (1,860m) → Mandara Hut (2,700m)
8km3-4 hrs+840mRainforest
Your climb begins at Marangu Gate on Kilimanjaro's southeastern flank, where your crew handles registration and park formalities while you take in your first views of the forest canopy. The trail climbs gently and steadily beneath a thick rainforest ceiling, alive with the calls of colobus and blue monkeys swinging through the branches overhead, giant ferns dripping with humidity, and towering podocarpus trees draped in old man's beard moss. This is one of the most comfortable opening days on the mountain — a well-graded, wide path that eases your legs into the days ahead. You'll arrive at Mandara Hut by mid-afternoon, with time to rest in your private A-frame cabin, enjoy a hot dinner prepared by your mountain cook, and take an optional short detour to the Maundi Crater viewpoint for sweeping views over the Kenyan plains before sunset.
2
Mandara Hut → Horombo Hut (3,720m)
12km6-7 hrs+1,020mMoorland
Today the landscape transforms dramatically as you climb out of the rainforest and into the heather and moorland zone, where giant lobelia and towering groundsel plants stand like something from another planet. The gradient is more sustained today, and the air noticeably thins, so your guide will set a deliberately unhurried “pole pole” (slowly slowly) pace to help your body begin adjusting to altitude. On clear afternoons, Kibo's glaciated summit and the jagged spires of Mawenzi Peak both come into view for the first time, a genuinely awe-inspiring moment for most climbers. Horombo Hut, perched on a ridge with huge panoramic views, is your home for the night — and on the 6-day itinerary, also the following night, giving your body crucial extra time to acclimatize before the harder days ahead.
3
Acclimatization Day at Horombo Hut (6-Day Itinerary)
4-5km2-3 hrsRest DayMoorland
This extra day is, quite simply, the single biggest factor in improving your chances of a successful summit on the Marangu route. Rather than pushing straight on toward Kibo, you'll take a shorter, gentle acclimatization walk up toward Zebra Rocks or the base of Mawenzi, gaining altitude during the day before returning to sleep at the lower elevation of Horombo Hut — the classic “climb high, sleep low” strategy used by experienced mountaineers worldwide. You'll have a relaxed afternoon to rest, hydrate, eat well and mentally prepare, with your guide checking in on how your body is responding to the altitude so far. Climbers who add this day consistently report feeling stronger and more confident heading into the harder terrain above.
4
Horombo Hut → Kibo Hut (4,700m)
10km6 hrs+980mAlpine Desert
Leaving the last of the moorland vegetation behind, you'll cross “The Saddle” — a vast, stark, high-altitude plateau stretching between Mawenzi and Kibo peaks, where almost nothing grows and the landscape feels closer to the surface of the moon than anywhere on Earth. The thin air and increasing cold make this a mentally demanding stretch, and your guide will keep the pace deliberately slow to conserve energy for what lies ahead. Kibo Hut itself is a stone structure sitting directly beneath the crater rim — basic, cold, and purposeful. You'll have an early dinner, lay out your summit gear, and try to rest (most climbers don't sleep much) before a midnight wake-up call for the push to the roof of Africa.
5
Summit Night: Kibo Hut → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Horombo Hut
21km total12-15 hrs+1,195m then descendSummit Push
You'll be woken around 11pm–midnight for tea, biscuits, and a final gear check before setting off into the freezing darkness under a canopy of stars. The path zig-zags relentlessly up loose scree to Gilman's Point (5,681m) on the crater rim — the toughest, slowest, most mentally challenging section of the entire climb — before a further 1–2 hours along the crater edge, past the last remaining glaciers, to Uhuru Peak itself, timed for a sunrise arrival that climbers describe as one of the most emotional moments of their lives. After photos at the famous summit sign, you'll begin the long descent, retracing your steps through Kibo and continuing all the way down to Horombo Hut — a brutally long day covering both the summit push and a huge descent, but one that ends with a proper meal, a real bed, and the deep satisfaction of having stood on Africa's highest point.
6
Horombo Hut → Marangu Gate
20km5-6 hrs-1,860mRainforest
The final day is a long, knee-testing descent retracing your route back through moorland and into the cool rainforest you climbed through on day one, though now with the satisfaction of the summit behind you. Porters and guides often break into song on this last stretch — a joyful Kilimanjaro tradition worth savoring. At Marangu Gate, you'll sign the official park register, receive your summit certificate (gold for Uhuru Peak, green for Gilman's Point), and say your thank-yous and tips to the crew who supported you throughout, before transfer back to your hotel in Arusha or Moshi for a well-earned hot shower and celebratory meal.

6 or 7 Days
Machame Route

The "Whiskey" route is the most scenic way up Kilimanjaro, climbing through rainforest, moorland, alpine desert and the dramatic Barranco Wall before reaching the summit. It’s camping-based throughout and steeper than Marangu, so fitness matters — on 7 days it offers one of the best summit success rates of any route.

Full Day-by-Day Itinerary
1
Machame Gate (1,800m) → Machame Camp (3,000m)
11km5-7 hrs+1,200mRainforest
The Machame route begins on Kilimanjaro's southern slopes, and after registration formalities at the gate, you'll plunge straight into dense, misty rainforest thick with hanging lichen, giant ferns and the occasional rustle of colobus monkeys overhead. The trail is muddy and root-strewn in places, especially if it has rained recently, so trekking poles earn their keep early. It's a genuinely beautiful walk, but also a serious first day — you'll gain over 1,200m in altitude, so pacing matters more than speed. By the time you reach Machame Camp, the forest has begun thinning into heath, and your crew will have tents pitched and hot drinks ready as the clouds roll in below you for a spectacular sunset above the cloud line.
2
Machame Camp → Shira Camp (3,840m)
5km4-6 hrs+840mMoorland
Although shorter in distance, today's climb is steep and rocky, following a ridge line up out of the last of the forest and onto open moorland dotted with giant groundsel and everlasting flowers. This is the day Kibo's glaciated summit first comes fully into view, often stopping climbers in their tracks for photos. Shira Camp sits on the vast, ancient Shira Plateau — the collapsed caldera of one of Kilimanjaro's three volcanic cones — and offers a genuinely spectacular sense of exposure and scale, with 360° views across the plateau to Kibo on one side and the plains of Tanzania far below on the other.
3
Shira Camp → Lava Tower (4,630m) → Barranco Camp (3,900m)
10km6-8 hrsthen down to 3,900mAlpine Desert
This is the classic “climb high, sleep low” acclimatization day that makes Machame such an effective route for summit success. You'll trek across increasingly barren alpine desert to the dramatic Lava Tower — a 300m volcanic plug that looms like a fortress — where you'll stop for a hot lunch at 4,630m, higher than many climbers have ever been before descending. From there, the trail drops through the strange and beautiful Barranco Valley, filled with giant senecio plants that look prehistoric against the rock, down to Barranco Camp at the foot of the imposing Barranco Wall. Sleeping nearly 700m lower than your lunch altitude gives your body vital time to recover and adapt.
4
Barranco Wall → Karanga Camp (3,995m)
5km4-5 hrsScrambleRidge
The day opens with the single most exciting stretch of the whole climb: the Barranco Wall, a 300m near-vertical scramble up volcanic rock that looks intimidating from camp but is entirely non-technical — hands-on in places, with your guide pointing out every foothold, but requiring no ropes or climbing gear. Reaching the top rewards you with some of the best views on the entire mountain, with the summit glaciers glinting directly ahead. From there, the trail rolls across a series of ridges and valleys, crossing streams fed by melting ice, before a final climb to Karanga Camp — a relatively short but memorable day that leaves plenty of afternoon for rest and photography.
5
Karanga Camp → Barafu Camp (4,673m)
4km4 hrs+678mAlpine Desert
A deliberately short, steady day across increasingly barren and rocky terrain, giving your body one last chance to rest and adjust before the main event. Barafu — Swahili for “ice” — is a stark, windswept, boulder-strewn camp perched on a narrow ridge, and it's here that the reality of the coming summit night really sinks in. You'll have an early, hearty dinner, lay out every piece of summit clothing and gear with your guide's guidance, and try to grab a few hours of sleep before the midnight wake-up call, with excitement (and a little nervous energy) running through camp.
6
Summit Night: Barafu Camp → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Mweka Camp (3,100m)
17km total12-15 hrsSummit PushLong Descent
Around midnight, bundled in every layer you own, you'll set off by headlamp into freezing darkness, switch-backing steadily up loose scree toward Stella Point on the crater rim. This stretch is as much a mental challenge as a physical one — cold, dark, slow, and demanding real determination — but your guide's steady “pole pole” pace and encouragement carry most climbers through. From Stella Point, it's a further hour along the crater rim past ancient glaciers to Uhuru Peak, timed for sunrise over the African plains — an unforgettable, often emotional moment at the top of the continent. After summit photos, you'll descend rapidly back to Barafu for a short rest and hot meal, then continue all the way down through changing vegetation zones to Mweka Camp in the forest — an exhausting but deeply rewarding day covering enormous distance and altitude change.
7
Mweka Camp → Mweka Gate (7-Day Itinerary)
10km3-4 hrs-1,573mRainforest
A gentle, celebratory final descent through lush rainforest back to Mweka Gate, where the change in air pressure and oxygen with every step down is a welcome relief on tired legs. At the gate, you'll sign the official park register and receive your summit certificate, followed by the customary tipping ceremony for your guide, cook and porter team — often accompanied by singing and dancing that makes for one of the most joyful moments of the whole trip. We build this extra 7th day into our standard itinerary rather than rushing the descent on summit day, giving your body and your memories room to breathe.

8 Days
Lemosho Route

Lemosho starts from the remote western side of the mountain, crossing the wide-open Shira Plateau before joining the summit route. Its extra distance and days give your body more time to acclimatize, which is why it consistently records some of the highest summit success rates — ideal if you want a quieter trail and the best odds of reaching Uhuru Peak.

Full Day-by-Day Itinerary
1
Londorossi Gate → Mti Mkubwa Camp (2,750m)
6km3-4 hrs+750mRainforest
Your Lemosho adventure begins with a scenic drive to the remote Londorossi Gate on the mountain's western side, well off the busier southern approach routes. After registration, a short 4x4 transfer drops you at the trailhead itself, from where you enter pristine, untouched rainforest — among the most beautiful and least-crowded forest walking anywhere on Kilimanjaro. Look out for colobus monkeys, forest birds and, if you're fortunate, fresh elephant or buffalo tracks crossing the trail, since this western flank sees genuine wildlife traffic. Mti Mkubwa (“Big Tree”) Camp is named for the towering ancient specimen that shades the campsite, a wonderfully atmospheric first night on the mountain.
2
Mti Mkubwa → Shira 1 Camp (3,500m)
8km5-6 hrs+750mMoorland
The forest gradually thins into heather and moorland as the trail climbs steadily onto the edge of the vast Shira Plateau, one of the largest calderas in the world and the collapsed remnant of Kilimanjaro's oldest volcanic cone. Giant groundsel and lobelia punctuate the increasingly open landscape, and on a clear day this is where Kibo's summit dome first reveals itself in full, often eliciting gasps from first-time climbers. Shira 1 Camp sits right on the plateau's edge, with vast, uninterrupted views back across the plains of Tanzania — a genuinely spectacular sense of space and scale rarely matched elsewhere on the mountain.
3
Shira 1 → Shira 2 Camp (3,850m)
10km4 hrs+350mPlateau
A comparatively gentle, mostly flat day crossing the full width of the ancient Shira Plateau, ideal for easy acclimatization without excessive exertion. The walking is relaxed enough to properly take in the otherworldly high-altitude scenery — rolling volcanic grassland stretching toward Kibo's ever-closer summit — and your guide may point out interesting volcanic rock formations and hardy alpine plant species adapted to the thin air and intense UV exposure up here. Shira 2 Camp offers superb, unobstructed views of the summit for sunset, a favorite photography spot on the whole route.
4
Shira 2 → Lava Tower (4,630m) → Barranco Camp (3,900m)
10km6 hrsthen down to 3,900mAlpine Desert
Today brings the pivotal “climb high, sleep low” acclimatization day shared with the Machame route once the two trails converge. You'll cross increasingly barren volcanic terrain to reach the dramatic Lava Tower, a striking 300m rock formation where you'll stop for a well-earned hot lunch at just over 4,600m — a genuinely valuable altitude exposure before you descend. From there the trail drops into the strange, beautiful Barranco Valley, filled with towering prehistoric-looking senecio plants, ending at Barranco Camp beneath the imposing wall you'll tackle first thing tomorrow. The nearly 700m of descent to camp gives your body crucial recovery time.
5
Barranco Wall → Karanga Camp (3,995m)
5km5 hrsScrambleRidge
The day begins with the famous Barranco Wall — a thrilling, non-technical scramble up 300m of volcanic rock that looks far more intimidating from below than it feels once you're climbing it hand over hand alongside your guide. The reward at the top is arguably the best panoramic view on the entire mountain, with Kibo's glaciers close enough to feel almost touchable. From there, a scenic but tiring route crosses several ridges and valleys, with streams to cross and steady altitude gain, before reaching Karanga Camp — the last reliable water source before the summit push, so your crew will top up supplies here.
6
Karanga Camp → Barafu Camp (4,673m)
4km4 hrs+678mAlpine Desert
A short, steady climb across stark, rocky alpine desert to Barafu Camp, your final staging point before summit night. With relatively little distance to cover today, you'll have a proper rest afternoon — time to sort and repack summit gear with your guide, hydrate thoroughly, and try to nap, even if genuine sleep proves elusive at this altitude. The mood in camp shifts noticeably here, a mix of nervous anticipation and quiet determination as everyone prepares mentally for the night ahead.
7
Summit Night: Barafu Camp → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Mweka Camp (3,100m)
17km total12-15 hrsSummit PushLong Descent
Waking around midnight, you'll begin the long, cold, switch-backing ascent by headlamp toward Stella Point on the crater rim — slow, deliberate steps guided by your team's experience and encouragement through the hardest hours of the entire trek. From Stella Point, a further hour along the rim past ancient glacial ice leads to Uhuru Peak, Africa's highest point, typically reached right around sunrise for a genuinely unforgettable view over the curvature of the earth. Thanks to Lemosho's extra acclimatization days on the Shira Plateau, climbers on this route consistently report feeling stronger here than on shorter itineraries. After summit celebrations, you'll descend all the way back through Barafu to Mweka Camp in the forest — a huge, demanding day, but one that ends with the climb essentially behind you.
8
Mweka Camp → Mweka Gate
10km3 hrs-1,573mRainforest
A relatively gentle final descent through the forest back to Mweka Gate, where oxygen-rich air and warmer temperatures return with every step. You'll complete official park sign-out formalities, receive your summit certificate, and take part in the traditional tipping and thank-you ceremony for your guide, cook and porter crew — usually a warm, celebratory affair full of songs. From here it's a short drive back to your hotel in Arusha or Moshi for a hot shower and a very well-earned rest.

6 Days
Rongai Route

Rongai is the only route that approaches Kilimanjaro from the north, near the Kenyan border, through dry pine forest and open moorland. It sees far fewer trekkers than the southern routes, has a gradual gradient, and is a solid choice during the rainy season since the northern slopes stay drier.

Full Day-by-Day Itinerary
1
Rongai Gate (1,950m) → Simba Camp (2,600m)
8km3-4 hrs+650mPine Forest
Rongai is the only route that approaches Kilimanjaro from the quieter, drier northern side near the Kenyan border, and the difference is obvious from the very first steps. Rather than dense rainforest, you'll walk through open pine forest and farmland, with local Chagga villages and maize fields visible in the distance — a genuinely different cultural and scenic experience from the southern routes. The trail is gentle and the crowds virtually nonexistent, making this a wonderfully peaceful start. Simba (“Lion”) Camp takes its name from tracks occasionally spotted in the area, and offers your first proper views of Kibo's northern glaciers as dusk falls.
2
Simba Camp → Kikelewa Camp (3,600m)
8km6-7 hrs+1,000mMoorland
The trail climbs steadily out of the forest onto open, rolling moorland, with sweeping views north across the plains toward Kenya and, on the clearest days, all the way to Mount Kenya itself far in the distance. This is a longer, more demanding day in terms of altitude gain, so the pace is deliberately unhurried and regular water breaks are built in. Kikelewa Camp is tucked into a scenic valley below the jagged spires of Mawenzi Peak, with wildflowers often blooming nearby depending on the season — one of the most photogenic camps on the whole mountain.
3
Kikelewa Camp → Mawenzi Tarn (4,330m)
5km4 hrs+730mAlpine
A short but steep climb brings you directly beneath the dramatic, jagged spires of Mawenzi — Kilimanjaro's second peak and a striking contrast to Kibo's smooth glaciated dome. Mawenzi Tarn Camp, set beside a small alpine lake, is widely considered one of the most spectacular and least-visited campsites on the entire mountain, and the shorter walking distance today gives you a valuable half-day to rest and properly acclimatize before pushing on toward Kibo. Many climbers rate this as their favorite camp of the whole trip for its scenery alone.
4
Mawenzi Tarn → Kibo Hut (4,700m)
9km4-5 hrs+370mAlpine Desert
Today you cross “The Saddle” — the vast, stark high-altitude plateau stretching between Mawenzi and Kibo, where the landscape turns almost entirely to rock and dust and barely a plant survives the thin, freezing air. It's a mentally demanding stretch, both for its bleakness and the growing awareness of the summit push ahead, but the walking itself is relatively flat and manageable. Kibo Hut, a stone shelter sitting right beneath the crater rim, is basic and cold by design — this is a place for an early dinner, careful gear preparation, and an attempt at rest before the midnight wake-up call.
5
Summit Night: Kibo Hut → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Horombo Hut
21km total13-15 hrsSummit PushLong Descent
Setting off around midnight, you'll climb steadily by headlamp up loose volcanic scree toward Gilman's Point on the crater rim — the toughest, coldest, most demanding hours of the entire trek, made bearable by your guide's steady pace and encouragement. From Gilman's Point, the trail continues along the crater edge, past remnant glaciers glowing faintly in the pre-dawn light, to Uhuru Peak itself, usually timed for a sunrise arrival that few climbers ever forget. After summit photos at Africa's highest point, the descent begins in earnest — back down through Kibo Hut and continuing all the way to Horombo Hut, an exceptionally long day covering both the summit push and a major descent, but one that ends with real food, warmth and the summit already behind you.
6
Horombo Hut → Marangu Gate
20km6 hrs-1,770mRainforest
The final day retraces the descent through moorland and back into cool, green rainforest, joining the Marangu route path for the exit (Rongai climbers descend via Marangu Gate rather than returning the way they came). It's a long day on tired legs, but spirits are typically high, with porters and guides often singing traditional Kilimanjaro songs on the way down. At the gate you'll complete official sign-out procedures, receive your summit certificate, and enjoy a proper tipping and thank-you ceremony with your crew before transfer back to Arusha or Moshi.

Umbwe Route

Umbwe is the shortest and steepest route to the summit, climbing straight through dense forest onto a narrow ridge with dramatic views before meeting the Barranco Wall. It’s camping-only with no easy escape route, so we only recommend it to fit, experienced trekkers looking for a tougher, more dramatic climb.

Full Day-by-Day Itinerary
1
Umbwe Gate (1,600m) → Umbwe Cave Camp (2,940m)
11km5-6 hrs+1,340mDense Forest
Umbwe is Kilimanjaro's most direct and demanding route, and that reputation is obvious from the very first day: a steep, relentless climb through dense, beautiful forest along a narrow ridge trail, gaining well over 1,300m in a single push. There's minimal traffic on this route — you'll likely have the trail almost entirely to yourselves — and the forest itself is genuinely spectacular, thick with moss-draped trees and the calls of unseen wildlife. Umbwe Cave Camp is a small, atmospheric clearing tucked into the forest, and by the time you arrive most climbers feel the day's effort in their legs, a clear sign this route is built for fitter, more experienced trekkers.
2
Umbwe Cave Camp → Barranco Camp (3,900m)
5km4-5 hrs+960mRidge
The climb continues up out of the forest and onto an increasingly exposed ridge, with the trees thinning to reveal your first proper views of Kibo's Southern Icefields glinting in the distance — a genuinely dramatic reward after yesterday's forest slog. The trail here can be narrow and occasionally requires careful footing, particularly after rain, so a measured pace matters. Barranco Camp, where Umbwe rejoins the Machame/Lemosho route, sits directly beneath the imposing Barranco Wall you'll climb the following morning, and offers spectacular sunset views across the valley.
3
Barranco Wall → Karanga Camp (3,995m)
5km4-5 hrsScrambleRidge
A thrilling, hands-on (but non-technical) scramble up the 300m Barranco Wall kicks off the day, rewarding climbers with arguably the finest panoramic views on the whole mountain from the top. From there, the trail follows the same scenic ridge-and-valley route used by Machame and Lemosho climbers, crossing several small streams before reaching Karanga Camp — the last water source before the summit push, and a good spot to rest and recover ahead of the demanding days to come.
4
Karanga Camp → Barafu Camp (4,673m)
4km4 hrs+678mAlpine Desert
A shorter, steadier day across stark alpine terrain to Barafu Camp, your final staging point before the summit attempt. Because Umbwe compresses the acclimatization schedule used on longer routes, your guide will monitor how you're feeling especially closely today, and this is the point where we most often recommend adding an extra day if time allows. Early dinner, thorough gear checks, and an attempt at rest follow, ahead of a midnight start.
5
Summit Night: Barafu Camp → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Mweka Camp
17km total12-15 hrsSummit PushLong Descent
The summit push follows the same demanding path used by Machame and Lemosho climbers — a steep, cold, headlamp-lit ascent via Stella Point to Uhuru Peak, ideally timed for sunrise. Because Umbwe skips the gentler acclimatization built into longer routes, this is a genuinely tough night, and we only recommend this route to fit, experienced trekkers for exactly this reason. After summiting, the descent continues all the way down to Mweka Camp in the forest — an enormous, exhausting day, but one that puts the hardest part of the climb firmly behind you.
6
Mweka Camp → Mweka Gate (Recommended Extension)
10km3 hrs-1,573mRainforest
Rather than rushing straight from the summit to the gate on the same exhausting day, we recommend this gentler final descent through rainforest back to Mweka Gate on its own separate day. It's a far more comfortable way to finish, with time to properly register the achievement, complete official sign-out and certificate formalities, and enjoy a proper tipping and thank-you ceremony with your crew before the drive back to Moshi or Arusha.

3 Days
Mount Meru — 3 Days

A brisk two-night climb through Arusha National Park, starting among buffalo, giraffe and colobus monkeys near Momella Gate before rising through montane forest to Meru’s dramatic crater rim. It’s a fast, physical ascent, and a fantastic way to acclimatize before attempting Kilimanjaro.

Full Day-by-Day Itinerary
1
Momella Gate (1,500m) → Miriakamba Hut (2,514m)
10km4-5 hrs+1,014mWildlife
Your Meru adventure begins inside Arusha National Park, where an armed ranger joins your group for the walk — a requirement here given the genuine wildlife you're likely to encounter, unlike the wildlife-free trails of Kilimanjaro. The route winds through open grassland and acacia woodland where buffalo, giraffe, warthog and colobus monkeys are commonly spotted at close range, with Meru's steep, forested slopes rising ahead of you the whole way. It's a fantastic introduction to East African wildlife before you've even reached your first camp. Miriakamba Hut sits in a clearing with lovely views back across the plains, and offers simple but comfortable bunk accommodation for the night.
2
Miriakamba Hut → Saddle Hut (3,570m)
5km4-5 hrs+1,056mForest to Moorland
The trail climbs steadily through montane forest thick with old man's beard moss, then breaks out onto open heather and moorland as you approach the saddle between Meru's main summit and its dramatic ash cone. This stretch offers some of the best views on the entire climb, with the sheer inner wall of Meru's collapsed crater rising directly beside you — a genuinely dramatic geological sight most climbers aren't expecting. Saddle Hut, perched right on the exposed ridge, delivers sweeping views in every direction, including (on the clearest days) all the way across to Kilimanjaro itself in the distance.
3
Summit Night: Saddle Hut → Socialist Peak (4,566m) → Descent to Momella Gate
Very Long Day8-9 hrs to summit, plus full descentSummit PushRidge
This is a genuinely demanding final day: an early (often midnight) start along Meru's narrow, exposed summit ridge, with real drop-offs on either side in places, climbing steadily toward Socialist Peak at 4,566m. Reaching the top rewards you with one of the most striking summit views in East Africa, looking directly down into Meru's collapsed crater on one side and, on a clear morning, across to Kilimanjaro rising above the clouds on the other. After summit celebrations, the day continues with a very long descent all the way back down to Momella Gate — making this the single longest, most physically demanding day of the 3-day itinerary, and the main reason we often recommend the 4-day option instead for a gentler pace.

4 Days
Mount Meru — 4 Days

The same spectacular route up Mount Meru, spread over an extra night at Saddle Hut for a gentler pace and better acclimatization. Expect wildlife on the lower slopes, sweeping views of Kilimanjaro on a clear morning, and a sunrise summit push along Meru’s knife-edge ridge.

Full Day-by-Day Itinerary
1
Momella Gate (1,500m) → Miriakamba Hut (2,514m)
10km4-5 hrs+1,014mWildlife
The same wildlife-rich opening day as the 3-day option: an armed ranger accompanies your group through open grassland and acacia woodland where buffalo, giraffe and colobus monkeys are frequently spotted, with Meru's dramatic slopes visible ahead the entire way. It's an unusually scenic and wildlife-filled start for a mountain trek, and a great warm-up for the climbing to come. Miriakamba Hut offers comfortable bunk accommodation and lovely views back across the plains for your first night.
2
